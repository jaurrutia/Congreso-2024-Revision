## Spectral localization of plasmonic resonances in arbitrarily sized Drude-like nanospheres
 **D. Larissa Luna González**, J. A. Urrutia-Anguiano, A. Reyes-Coronado

**National Physics Congress 2024 (CNF 2024)**

**Format:** Poster  

**Presented: 10/10/2024  **

**Abstract:**  
**Nanoplasmonics** is the study of the electromagnetic response in systems with a metallic response at the nanoscale, meaning, with dimensions smaller than 100 nm. In systems spatially confined to this scale, the phenomenon of **localized surface plasmon resonance** occurs, resulting from the coupling between the free electrons of a metal and the incident electromagnetic field that illuminates the system. This phenomenon can be used in applications such as spectroscopy and medicine, due to the tuning of these resonances to a specific frequency according to the morphological properties of the system. **Mie theory** is an analytical solution to Maxwell's equations that describes the excitation of the surface plasmon resonance for spherical metallic particles illuminated by a plane electromagnetic wave through a sum of multipolar contributions of electric and magnetic modes, with which the extinction cross-section can be calculated, whose maximum corresponds to the excitation of the localized surface plasmon. In this work, the spectral localization of the plasmonic resonances excited in **spherical particles**, characterized by a dielectric function described by the **Drude model**, is studied theoretically and numerically as a function of their radius. To numerically determine the spectral position of the plasmonic resonances of the first electric and magnetic multipolar orders, the extinction cross-section is calculated for each of them, and the frequency that maximizes these quantities is identified. Finally, the obtained results are contrasted with the resonance condition in the **small particle limit** obtained analytically. Based on the results, curves of the redshift of the localized surface plasmon resonances for spherical particles are presented as the small particle limit ceases to be valid.
